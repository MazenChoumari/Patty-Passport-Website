THE PROBLEM WITH "MEDITERRANEAN FOOD"

Walk into most Mediterranean restaurants and you get an average: some hummus, some feta, a bit of harissa, olives on everything. Twenty-one distinct food cultures flattened into one beige idea of "sunny".

But Lebanese garlic sauce has nothing to do with Croatian ajvar. A Tunisian brik is not a Spanish tortilla. Bosnian ćevapi and Greek souvlaki both come off a grill and share nothing else.

So we stopped averaging. Every country gets its own plate, its own page, its own playlist and its own stamp — and guests travel between them one visit at a time.
