Where we sit against the category

THE POSITIONING MAP.

Fast-food chains win on convenience and price but stay low on immersion. Premium burger brands raise the price without adding a world to sit inside. Patty Passport sits in the upper-middle: a fair, accessible price with a destination-level experience wrapped around it — more immersive than ordinary casual dining, more accessible than premium experiential dining.

Honest self-assessment

SWOT.

Strengths
  • Twenty-one destination identities in one repeatable format
  • Passport and stamp system encourage return visits
  • Works for families, birthdays, schools and teams
  • Strong visual world: terminal, routes, maps and cards
  • Shared ingredients can support operational efficiency
  • Content can be reused across locations
Weaknesses
  • Menu and story complexity require serious training
  • Country authenticity must be maintained at scale
  • Large footprint and build-out increase capital needs
  • Broad menu can create stock and preparation complexity
  • Experience quality depends on consistent crew execution
Opportunities
  • Second Madrid location
  • Events and private group bookings
  • School and educational partnerships
  • Kids programming
  • Country nights and seasonal menus
  • Merchandise and passport extensions
  • Destination content and membership
  • Expansion into Mediterranean-facing cities
Threats
  • Burger and casual-dining imitation
  • Food, labour and construction inflation
  • Weak execution damaging trust
  • Permitting and site delays
  • Consumer spending pressure
  • Competitors copying parts of the experience
  • Dietary or allergen mistakes

Planning targets, not guaranteed outcomes

01 Open and stabilize the first terminal during 2026.
02 Establish the complete 21-country rotation, passport system and event calendar in year one.
03 Measure repeat visits, passport completion, average spend, event conversion and guest satisfaction monthly.
04 Validate a second Madrid location in 2027 using the first site's operating data.
05 Prepare standardized training, menu systems and content libraries before multi-site expansion.

These are planning targets set by the founders, not guaranteed outcomes or dates committed to investors.

How this grows, and what it isn't

GROWTH STRATEGY.

The Ansoff growth matrix — tap a quadrant for what it actually means here.

Porter's generic strategies

Focused differentiation — not cost leadership.

Patty Passport doesn't try to be the cheapest burger in the category — fast-food chains already own that position on scale nobody can match without it. The strategy is a focused segment (guests who want a destination-level experience with their meal) served through differentiation (the passport, the routes, the 21-country menu) rather than through price.

That's also why the price sits above fast-food and below premium experiential dining on the positioning map above — differentiation funds the experience; it isn't funded by being the lowest price in the category.

WHY THIS DEFENDS ITSELF

FM Format, not recipe A competitor can copy one burger. Copying twenty-one countries plus the passport plus the zones is a different project.
RT Retention by design The book gives a reason to come back twenty more times. Discount loyalty gives one.
CT Reusable content Playlists, cards and booklets produced once ship free to every future site.
SU Single supply chain Shared proteins and bases across all twenty-one plates keep COGS flat as variety grows.
BR Brand with a world Passport, terminal, crew, routes — a language guests understand on arrival.
